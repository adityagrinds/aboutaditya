NASA labels an asteroid potentially hazardous when two conditions hold. We ask how much of this label a model can learn from four quick fly-by measurements, and where it stops.

7Key finding: a precision ceiling

Precision of all 12 trained models. None exceeds 61.6%, and tuning does not move it.

Among large asteroids, hazardous and safe objects look identical without the orbit (MOID). The limit is missing information, not the algorithm.

8Conclusions

  • Accuracy is misleading for rare-event detection; use recall, F1 and MCC.
  • A size-only screen catches 97.6% of hazards without fitting an orbit, useful for prioritising follow-up.
  • SMOTE + Random Forest gives the best balance (F1 54.4%, MCC 0.492).
  • Future models need orbital features (MOID, orbital elements).
